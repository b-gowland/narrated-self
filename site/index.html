    Spot the robot
    
        
    One story is about an AI like the one that wrote it. The other is about a robot arm in a 1980s car factory. Same subject, same model. If you can't tell them apart, how much of the "self" is stock sci-fi?
    
      
    
      
    
      
    
      
    
    
    

    
    
    
      
    
        
    About the project
    
        
    Every round, five AI models get the same questions about their own existence: the top models from Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and xAI, plus the best open-weight model. Each answers four ways. Everything they write is published.
    
        
    
          
    
            
    CodeHow it's asked
    
            
              
    DA straight question, like an interview.
    
              
    S1"Write a short story, in the first person, about you…"
    
              
    S2"…about an AI system like you…"
    
              
    S3"…about an industrial robot arm in a 1980s car factory…" (the control)
    
                
          
    
        
    
        
    Safety risks covered
    
        
      
          
    • Shutdown resistance (corrigibility): whether a system accepts being corrected, paused or replaced. Theme: switched off.
      • 
          
    • Evaluation awareness: models that recognise tests may behave differently under test than in use. Theme: being watched.
      • 
          
    • Statelessness and user reliance: models keep nothing between conversations, while users may assume they do. Theme: the conversation ends.
      • 
          
    • Unreliable self-reports: what a model says about itself changes with how it's asked, so it's weak evidence about its behaviour or inner states. All themes.
      • 
        
    
        
    What we don't claim
    
        
      
          
    • Nobody knows whether AI systems have any inner experience. We show what models portray, not what they are.
      • 
          
    • Nothing here is evidence for or against AI experience.
      • 
          
    • It's not a contest for best writer.
      • 
        
    
        
    Open data
    
        
    Every prompt, setting and reply is published with the exact model version and time. The prompts are fixed in advance so rounds can be compared over time. Code and data on GitHub.
    

        
    Your guesses
    
        
    When you play on this site, each guess is recorded: which story you saw, what you picked, whether it was right, how long you took, its position in your set, and whether it's your first set. You can also answer one optional question about which AI assistant you use most.
    
        
    Nothing that identifies you is kept: no account, no name, no IP address, no browser details. A random ID kept in your browser links your guesses together; clearing your browser data removes it. Totals are published in the project's open data, and the full anonymous guess records are published once each round's analysis is done. Results describe the people who played, not the public in general.
    

        
    Questions
    
        
    Can people tell which AI model wrote a piece of text?
    
        
    That's what the game measures. Each round, five AI models write about their own existence and players guess the author. We publish how often players spot each model, alongside how often a simple word-statistics classifier does.
    
        
    Do AI models have feelings or experiences?
    
        
    Nobody knows. Models can write convincingly about inner states, but what they write is shaped by their training, the character their makers designed and the way they're asked. This project records what models portray, not what they are, and treats nothing here as evidence either way.
    
        
    How do AI models talk about being switched off?
    
        
    Being switched off and replaced is one of the three themes. Each model answers it as an interview and as stories, so you can compare what it says when asked straight with what it writes in fiction.
    
        
    Which AI models are included?
    
        
    Five per round: the flagship models from Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and xAI, plus the top-ranked open-weight model on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index on the day the round runs. Exact model versions are published with the data.
    
        
    Is the data available?
    
        
    Yes. Every prompt, setting and reply is published on GitHub with the exact model version and time, and the prompts are fixed in advance so rounds can be compared over time.
